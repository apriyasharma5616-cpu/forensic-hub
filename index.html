Educational Case Studies

Examine realistic fictional case scenarios designed for first-year analytical reasoning. Evaluate physical evidence logs, trace transfers, and laboratory findings before revealing the investigator's scientific conclusion.

CASE #101-ARS Level: 1st Year Chemistry

The Midnight Warehouse Fire

Fire investigators responded to a sudden commercial storage facility fire. Charring was localized around low-lying baseboards with inverted 'V' patterns on drywall, indicating a low ignition origin.

Evidentiary Item Recovered:

Charred floor joists packaged in unlined clean metal paint cans. Passive headspace Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis detected distinctive alkane homologous series ($C_9$ through $C_{16}$).

Forensic Analysis: The alkane distribution and aromatic profile (toluene, xylenes, alkylbenzenes) correspond specifically to Medium Petroleum Distillates (diesel fuel / kerosene accelerant), refuting the claim of spontaneous electrical fault. The airtight metal can prevented evaporation of volatile hydrocarbons, securing crucial evidence for arson prosecution.
CASE #102-BIO Level: 1st Year Biology / Trace

The Whispering Velvet Fiber

A late-night burglary occurred through a shattered French door. An expensive vintage painting was stolen. The suspect wore disposable latex gloves to avoid leaving fingerprints on the frame.

Evidentiary Item Recovered:

Clear adhesive tape lifts applied to jagged glass edges yielded several triangular cross-section royal blue synthetic fibers with identical dye absorbance under microspectrophotometry.

Forensic Analysis: Demonstrates Locard’s Exchange Principle. While the suspect avoided fingerprint deposition by wearing gloves, friction against the broken glass snagged trilobal nylon fibers matching a customized jacket seized from the suspect's residence. Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) established identical refractive index and sign of elongation.
CASE #103-PHY Level: 1st Year Physics

The Corridor Projectile Trajectory

A dispute in a narrow residential corridor resulted in a firearm discharge into a plasterboard partition. Conflicting witness statements claimed the shot was fired either from an upward or downward angle.

Evidentiary Item Recovered:

An elliptical blood spatter droplet on the adjacent wall measured $w = 6.2\text{ mm}$ and $l = 12.4\text{ mm}$, accompanied by concentric bullet exit spalling on the drywall.

Forensic Analysis: Applying the trigonometric relationship $\theta = \arcsin(w/l) = \arcsin(6.2/12.4) = \arcsin(0.5) = 30^\circ$. String line trajectory reconstruction back through the drywall penetration demonstrated a downward trajectory of $30^\circ$, corroborating that the shooter was standing on the elevated staircase landing above the victim.
CASE #104-DIG Level: 1st Year Digital

The Encrypted Exfiltration Ghost

An insider trading suspect claimed they never accessed proprietary company acquisition dossiers prior to their resignation at 17:00 on Friday.

Evidentiary Item Recovered:

Forensic bit-stream clone (.E01) of the employee laptop verified with SHA-256 hash. Examination of Windows Shellbags, USBSTOR registry keys, and $UsnJrnl change logs.

Forensic Analysis: USBSTOR records confirmed insertion of a 64GB Kingston flash drive (Serial #001A4D) at 16:42. Prefetch execution artifacts for 7-Zip correlated with an encrypted zip creation timestamp. LNK shortcuts demonstrated direct folder traversal into the confidential acquisitions folder, conclusively disproving the defense.

TEST SPECIMEN: CRIME SCENE SWAB #CS-442

Kastle-Meyer Presumptive Assay

The Kastle-Meyer test exploits the peroxidase-like activity of hemoglobin in blood. Follow the exact sequential protocol in the lab:

  1. Ethanol: Lyses red blood cells and exposes iron-bearing heme groups.
  2. Phenolphthalin: Reduced colorless indicator. (Crucial check: if it turns pink immediately before step 3, chemical oxidants are present = false positive).
  3. Hydrogen Peroxide ($H_2O_2$): Substrate. Heme catalyzes $2H_2O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O + O_2$, oxidizing reagent to vibrant pink.

Professional Pathways

Careers in Forensic Science

Forensic science degrees unlock specialized careers across public crime laboratories, medical examiner offices, private analytical firms, and cyber response units.

FIELD INVESTIGATION

Crime Scene Investigator (CSI)

Deploys to active crime scenes to document physical geometry, perform forensic photography, develop latent prints in situ, and preserve physical evidence following strict chain of custody.

  • Scene perimeter management & evidence photography
  • Latent print powder & chemical visualization
  • Biological fluid collection & preservation
  • Expert witness testimony in criminal courts
Prerequisite: B.Sc. in Forensic Science, Chemistry, or Criminalistics with field certification.
LABORATORY ANALYTICAL

Forensic Toxicologist

Analyzes biological matrices (whole blood, vitreous humor, liver tissue, hair) to isolate, identify, and quantify poisons, alcohol, narcotics, and therapeutic drugs.

  • Operating GC-MS, LC-MS/MS, and HPLC spectrometers
  • Post-mortem toxicology for medical examiners
  • Driving Under the Influence (DUI) blood alcohol quantification
  • Quality assurance and strict validation protocols
Prerequisite: B.Sc. in Chemistry, Biochemistry, or Pharmacology with instrumental lab training.
BIOMETRICS & IMPRESSION

Latent Print Examiner

Specializes in friction ridge analysis on physical evidence using optical, physical, and chemical processing techniques, followed by ACE-V comparison with AFIS/NGI databases.

  • Chemical enhancement with Cyanoacrylate and Ninhydrin
  • Level 1, 2, and 3 minutiae feature mapping
  • Automated Fingerprint Identification System searching
  • Peer-reviewed verification of individualizations
Prerequisite: B.Sc. in Forensic Science or Biology with IAI Latent Print Certification pathway.
CYBERSECURITY & DIGITAL

Digital Forensics Analyst

Recovers and parses digital evidence from confiscated smartphones, computers, vehicle telematics, and cloud servers to investigate cyberattacks, fraud, and homicide cases.

  • Bit-stream forensic cloning & hash verification
  • Volatile RAM memory extraction & artifact analysis
  • Deleted file carving & SQLite database reconstruction
  • Malware reverse-engineering and timeline analysis
Prerequisite: B.Sc. in Digital Forensics, Computer Science, or Cybersecurity with EnCE/GCFE training.
MOLECULAR GENETICS

Forensic DNA Analyst

Extracts, quantifies, amplifies, and profiles DNA from challenging trace samples (touch DNA, blood, bone) using multiplex PCR and capillary electrophoresis.

  • Differential extraction for sexual assault kits
  • STR multiplex typing across 20+ standard loci
  • Statistical calculation of random match probabilities
  • CODIS national database administration
Prerequisite: B.Sc. in Molecular Biology, Genetics, or Biochemistry with courses in Population Genetics.
BALLISTICS & TRACE

Firearms & Toolmark Examiner

Examines recovered firearms, fired bullets, cartridge cases, and toolmarks to determine mechanical function, muzzle-to-target distance, and striation matches.

  • Comparison microscopy of striation and breech marks
  • Chemical gunpowder residue mapping (Modified Griess)
  • Trajectory reconstruction and ejection pattern testing
  • NIBIN ballistics database correlation
Prerequisite: B.Sc. in Forensic Science, Physics, or Mechanical Engineering with AFTE apprenticeship.

Educational Mission

About Forensic Hub

Forensic Hub is an open, modern educational web platform dedicated to supporting first-year university students embarking on their degree studies in Forensic Science, Criminalistics, and Analytical Criminology.

First-year students frequently encounter a steep learning curve transitioning into university-level forensic coursework: integrating analytical chemistry with molecular biology, vector physics, and strict criminal jurisprudence. Forensic Hub bridges textbook theory with active visualization, interactive virtual laboratory simulations, and peer academic exchange.

  • Peer-Driven Open Pedagogy Built to democratize access to foundational forensic laboratory protocols, study flashcards, and analytical calculators.
  • Simulated Practical Exercises Safe, interactive digital environments to understand colorimetric reactions (Kastle-Meyer), gel migrations, and minutiae extraction.
Independent Educational Resource Disclaimer: Forensic Hub is an independent, non-commercial open educational project created solely for peer learning, undergraduate study revision, and science communication. CORE CURRICULUM VALUES

The Forensic Ethos

1. Objectivity & Scientific Integrity

Forensic scientists are advocates for the physical evidence itself, never for the prosecution or defense.

2. Reproducibility & Validation

Every analytical methodology must satisfy strict Daubert or Frye admissibility criteria through rigorous control testing.

3. Unbroken Chain of Custody

Physical integrity is only as sound as the documentation proving where evidence has been at every second.

4. Continuous Learning

From next-generation DNA sequencing to AI-assisted video forensics, scientific vigilance never ceases.
